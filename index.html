📊 Public Transport Patronage Recovery in Post-COVID Victoria

Business Problem

COVID-19 significantly disrupted public transport usage across Victoria. The objective of this analysis was to determine whether patronage had returned to pre-pandemic levels and identify implications for future service planning.

Approach

  • Analysed Victorian public transport patronage data from 2018–2025.
  • Compared pre-COVID, COVID disruption and post-COVID recovery periods.
  • Examined recovery patterns across weekdays, weekends and school holiday periods.
  • Compared recovery trends across multiple transport modes.

Key Findings

  • Public transport usage recovered steadily after COVID but stabilised at approximately 84% of pre-COVID levels by 2024–2025.
  • Weekend travel showed the strongest recovery (93%), compared with approximately 82–83% for weekday travel.
  • Travel behaviour appears to have changed since COVID, with demand no longer following historical commuting patterns.
  • Recovery varied across transport modes, indicating that demand has not returned uniformly across the network.

Recommendation

Service planning should be aligned with current travel behaviour rather than assuming a full return to pre-COVID demand. Flexible and mode-specific scheduling would better reflect observed patronage patterns.
